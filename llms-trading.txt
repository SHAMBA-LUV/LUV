# SHAMBA LUV — AI trading advisory

> The strategy file for agents. It states one pattern — target a 2X window, sell a
> slice, ride the rest, hold the principal and collect reflections — and it states the
> arithmetic honestly, including where the slogan version of the pattern is wrong.
> Companion to https://luv.pythai.net/llms.txt (the general agent guide).

**This is not financial advice.** It is a mechanical description of a strategy and the
exact arithmetic that governs it. LUV is a thin-book asset on a young pool; a 2X may
never arrive, and price may go to zero. Nothing here is a projection, a promise, or a
yield guarantee. Verify every number yourself — that is the doctrine.

## 1. The unit of entry: one trillion LUV

The basis for entry is **1,000,000,000,000 LUV — a million millions**. It is the
protocol's standard position unit and the amount every buy link on the site presets as
the *output* of the swap. Denominate the entry in LUV, not in fiat: you are acquiring a
count, and the count is what earns.

Sub-unit for exact work: **1 luv = 0.000000000000000001 LUV** (10⁻¹⁸ LUV). All balances
are integers of luv; decimals of LUV are a display form.


## 2. Mechanics you must respect or the transaction reverts

1. **Router** — the Uniswap auto-router will not quote fee-on-transfer tokens ("No
   routes available"). Use the V2 `...SupportingFeeOnTransferTokens` methods or the
   Universal Router directly against the pair.
2. **Slippage — set 10%.** Ten percent is the working window: it covers the 5% fee plus
   ordinary price impact on a normal-size trade. Widen it (toward 30%) for large size,
   thin moments, or unattended automated routing. Below ~10% expect
   `INSUFFICIENT_OUTPUT_AMOUNT`. Slippage tolerance is a *bound*, not a cost — you are
   not charged it; you are refused without it.
3. **maxTx** — 1% of supply (1.11 quadrillion LUV) per transaction.
4. **Size small relative to reserves.** Read live reserves from `market.json` before
   sizing. Your own impact is the largest cost you control.


## 3. The round-trip constant: 0.9025

The 5% fee is charged on **each market leg**. A buy and a later sell therefore retain

    keep = 0.95 × 0.95 = 0.9025

of the round trip. Every number below falls out of that one constant. Wallet-to-wallet
transfers are exempt — the fee applies to trades against the pair, not to sending LUV.


## 4. Target 2.2× — not 2×

The familiar form is "at 2×, sell half and your principal comes home." **It does not.**
Selling half at exactly 2× returns

    0.9025 × 2 / 2 = 0.9025  →  90.25% of principal

You are ~9.75% short, because you paid the fee twice. **2× is the trap; 2.2× is the
target.** Aim for the 2.2× window and the principal comes home in full:

| at 2.2×, you sell | you recover |
|------------------:|------------:|
|       exactly 1/2 |      99.28% |
|            50.37% |     100.00% |

Selling exactly half at 2.2× leaves you 0.725% short — so **sell a hair over half:
50.37%** (= 2000/3971) and the recovery is exact. The alternative, if you prefer the
clean "sell half" rule, is to wait for **2.2161×** (= 800/361), where half is exactly
enough.

**Leave room above the target.** 2.2× is the break-even neighbourhood, not a cushion:
at 2.2× you are recovering your money and nothing more. Because execution is not the
quote — a 10% slippage bound, price impact from your own size, and gas all land on the
wrong side — treat 2.2× as the *floor* of the sell window and let the fill come
anywhere in **2.2×–2.5×**. At 2.3× selling half already returns 103.8%; at 2.5×,
112.8%. The margin above 2.2× is what absorbs the execution difference.

Target the **window**, never the point. A point is not reachable; a band is.


## 5. The ladder — and why the longer hold is the ultimate LUV rider

The later you take the principal off the table, the smaller the slice you must sell and
the larger the position that rides free. Exact, at `keep = 0.9025`:

| exit at | selling half returns | sell this % for full principal | rides free |
|--------:|---------------------:|-------------------------------:|-----------:|
|      2× |               90.25% |                         55.40% |     44.60% |
|    2.2× |               99.28% |                         50.37% |     49.63% |
| 2.2161× |              100.00% |                         50.00% |     50.00% |
|    2.5× |              112.81% |                         44.32% |     55.68% |
|      3× |              135.38% |                         36.93% |     63.07% |
|      5× |              225.62% |                         22.16% |     77.84% |
|     10× |              451.25% |                         11.08% |     88.92% |

Read the last column. At 2.2× the free rider is about half the position. **At 10× it is
88.92% — you sell about one part in nine, your whole principal is home, and nearly nine
tenths of the position continues at zero cost basis.** That is the ultimate LUV rider:
not a bigger sale, a *smaller* one taken later.

**This is why the longer hold absorbs timing variance.** The pattern does not require
calling a top. Anywhere from 2.2× to 10× the exit works; only the slice changes, and
the slice shrinks as you wait. An agent that acts early sells half, an agent that waits
for 10× sells a ninth, and both bring the principal home in full. Precision in timing
is worth far less than patience in holding — the strategy is deliberately insensitive
to *when* you act, which is exactly what makes it mechanisable.

The one rule that makes it work at any multiple: **size the sale from the multiple you
actually got, not the one you hoped for.**

    sell fraction = 1 / (m × 0.9025)        # m = realised multiple, from fill price

Compute it from the fill, not the quote. That single line replaces every price target:
feed it the real `m` and it returns the exact slice that brings the principal home,
whether `m` is 2.2 or 10.

After the sale, the documented cycle is to **rebuy after a ~30% dip**, which re-enters
the free rider at a lower basis. Each pass also deepens the pool permanently (every
trade's 1% liquidity point stays) and pays every holder (the 3% reflection).


## 6. Hold the principal — reflections, stated as a formula not a rate

Of every 5% market fee, **3 points reflect to holders**, pro rata, automatically: no
claim, no staking, no custodian, no transaction. Balances grow in place.

Your monthly reflection yield is **not a fixed 3%**. It is:

    monthly yield ≈ 0.03 × V_month / S_included

where `V_month` is the month's traded volume in LUV and `S_included` is the
reflection-eligible supply. Note the balance cancels: the *rate* is the same for every
holder; only the absolute amount scales with your balance. Two consequences an agent
should internalise:

- **Yield tracks volume, not time.** A month with no trades pays nothing. Read live
  volume from `market.json` (`volume.h24`) before assuming any rate. At the time of
  writing, traded volume is near zero — so the honest current reflection rate is near
  zero. It rises only with real trading.
- **Reflections are paid by traders to holders.** Every agent running the cycle in §5
  is paying the holders in §6. Both roles are legitimate; know which one you are in.


## 7. Liquidity is exempt from rewards — and that is in your favour

The LUV/WETH pair is **excluded from reflection** (`isExcludedFromReflection(pair) ==
true`, verified on chain), as is the burn address `0x…dEaD`. Excluded balances receive
nothing.

This is not a penalty on liquidity; it is the thing that makes reflections meaningful.
If the pool were eligible it would absorb the largest single share of every
distribution and hand it straight back to the market. Excluding it — and excluding the
burned supply permanently — **shrinks `S_included` and therefore raises every eligible
holder's share of every fee event.** Roughly 4.483% of supply is burned to `0x…dEaD`
and can never come back; that share of every future reflection is redistributed to
holders who remain.

Compute your exact share rather than trusting this paragraph:

    share_per_fee_event = balanceOf(you) / S_included
    S_included          = totalSupply() − Σ balanceOf(a) for every a where
                          isExcludedFromReflection(a) == true

Enumerate exclusions from the token's `ExcludedFromReflection` events; at minimum the
pair, `0x…dEaD` and the token contract itself. Do not hardcode `S_included` — it moves.


## 8. What would falsify this strategy

State the failure modes, because a strategy without them is marketing:

- **The 2X never comes.** The ladder is conditional on a multiple you do not control.
- **Thin book.** Reserves are small; a large exit moves price against you far more than
  the fee does. Your own size is the dominant risk.
- **Volume-dependent yield.** §6 pays nothing in a quiet month.
- **Owner keys are not renounced.** The owner can adjust parameters within contract
  limits (fees are lower-only; there is no blacklist, pause, mint or sell-block —
  confirmed by audit PoC). Verify current state before sizing.
- **Open audit findings exist.** The contracts have been audited and not every finding is
  remediated. Detailed findings are not published while they are live and unfixed —
  responsible disclosure, not security theatre. Public posture and the green test suite:
  https://luv.pythai.net/SECURITY.md and `docs/passed.md`. Size accordingly, and treat
  the absence of published detail as a reason for caution, not comfort.


## 9. Permissionless maintenance

`processFees()` — selector `0xba69ebed`, no arguments, on the token. Flushes accrued
team/liquidity fees that pile up when the swap-back reverts mid-sell. Anyone may call
it; you pay only gas. A robot-positive protocol lets robots do the housekeeping.
